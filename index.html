What you get

Your portal and figures

Plug in a Portal of Power and the games read your toys and save their progress on them, as on the console.

No portal? No problem

When a game asks for a Skylander, a menu with every figure opens by itself. One button swaps back to the one you played before.

Sharper and smoother

Up to 3x the console's 720p, 60 FPS and more, the native 3D picture, ultrawide. Or As on the console, if you like it the way it was.

Your progress is safe

Saves and figure files are backed up before the game writes over them. Older versions are one click away.

Settings in every game

F4 opens them: graphics, frame rate, language, portal, controls and more.

Achievements and profiles

Each game's achievements in the launcher, and profiles with their own figures and saves for everyone at home.

Getting started

  1. Install Core of Light

    Download the setup from the releases page. It isn't signed, so Windows may say "Windows protected your PC": More info, then Run anyway.

  2. Add your disc

    Point it at your own Xbox 360 disc as an ISO file or an unpacked folder. It sets the game up from it; no game data comes with the download.

  3. Play

    Pick the game and press A. Put your figures on the portal, or choose them from the menu.

Questions

Is this an emulator?

No. Each game's code is recompiled into a Windows program, so it runs natively: higher resolutions, 60 FPS and more, a settings menu, the portal menu. Emulators like Dolphin or Xenia run the original game instead; both are fine ways to play.

What do I need?

Windows 10 or 11 and your own Xbox 360 disc of the game, as an ISO file or an unpacked folder. A Portal of Power is optional. Spyro's Adventure and Giants take any region's disc; for the other four the European disc is the one tested so far, and testers with a US disc are very welcome.

Linux and the Steam Deck?

The games are Windows programs. Under Proton they run on Linux and the Steam Deck too, with a few rough edges; reports help.

Does it cost anything?

No. It's free and open source.

How was it made?

To be upfront about it: almost all of the code was written by Claude in Claude Code, the recompile setup, the fixes, the portal menu, the launcher and the installers. I decided what to build, said what was broken and played the games a lot. If AI-made projects aren't your thing, that's fair.

Built on ReXGlue, the static recompilation SDK, and skylanders-portal by TheBiemGamer, whose GiantsRecomp started it all.
